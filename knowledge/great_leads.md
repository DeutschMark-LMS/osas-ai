# GREAT LEADS (MICHAEL MASTERSON / MARK FORD & JOHN FORD)

## THE CORE PREMISE
The easiest way to start any sales message is to write a "Great Lead." The lead is the first 100 to 600 words of a sales promotion (immediately following the headline). Its sole purpose is to hook the reader, establish the big idea, build intense emotional resonance, and compel them to read the rest of the message. The type of lead you choose depends entirely on the prospect's state of awareness (Eugene Schwartz's 5 States of Awareness). If you match the wrong lead to the wrong awareness level, the copy fails immediately.

## THE RULE OF ONE
Every successful sales promotion must be driven by a single, compelling idea. Weak copy tries to say too much. Masterful copy focuses all its energy on one focal point.
1. **One Good Idea:** A single, central concept that is easy to grasp, intellectually stimulating, and emotionally resonant. (Not a list of benefits).
2. **One Core Emotion:** The primary feeling you want the prospect to experience (e.g., greed, fear, anger, hope, betrayal, indignation). Don't mix fear with joy; pick the dominant driver.
3. **One Captivating Story:** A single, cohesive narrative that illustrates the core idea, proves the point, and evokes the core emotion.
4. **One Desirable Benefit:** The ultimate, primary advantage the prospect will gain. All other features and secondary benefits must support this primary outcome.
5. **One Inevitable Response:** The single, clear action you want the prospect to take at the end (buy the product, opt-in, call).

## THE 6 TYPES OF LEADS (FROM MOST DIRECT TO MOST INDIRECT)

### 1. THE OFFER LEAD
*   **Target Audience:** Most Aware (They know your product, they know they want it, they just need the deal to push them over the edge).
*   **Characteristics:** The most direct of all leads. It starts immediately with the offer, price, discounts, premiums, and guarantees. No buildup, no emotional storytelling required. It relies on the strength of the deal.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   The core product/service.
    *   The discount or special pricing.
    *   The premiums/bonuses (the "bribe").
    *   The guarantee.
    *   The deadline (urgency).
*   **Example:** "Get 50% off our flagship copywriting course today only. Plus, get these 3 bonuses worth $500, fully backed by our 365-day money-back guarantee."
*   **When to Use:** Retargeting campaigns, warm email lists, backend sales to existing customers, catalog sales, and flash sales.

### 2. THE PROMISE LEAD
*   **Target Audience:** Product-Aware (They know what you sell, they know the benefits, but they aren't sure it's the right choice for them yet).
*   **Characteristics:** Starts with a bold, explicit promise of the core benefit. Connects the product directly to the prospect's primary desire. It's less direct than the Offer Lead because it focuses on the *result* rather than the deal itself.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   A massive, highly desirable claim.
    *   Immediate proof to back up the claim (crucial for credibility).
    *   A clear connection to the product.
*   **Example:** "How to write copy that converts at 10% or higher—even if you've never written a single sales letter in your life. And we have the data from 10,000 students to prove it."
*   **When to Use:** When you have a strong, unique, and provable benefit that stands out in a crowded market. Often used for software, B2B services, and established solutions with a clear ROI.

### 3. THE PROBLEM-SOLUTION LEAD
*   **Target Audience:** Solution-Aware (They know the result they want, they know products exist to solve it, but they don't know your specific product).
*   **Characteristics:** Agitates the core problem, introduces a new or superior solution, and proves why it works better than what they've tried before. It acts as a bridge between their frustration and your unique mechanism.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   Intense identification with the prospect's pain or frustration.
    *   The failure of past solutions (why what they tried didn't work).
    *   The introduction of the *Unique Mechanism* (the real reason your product succeeds where others failed).
*   **Example:** "Tired of spending hours writing copy that barely converts? The problem isn't your writing—it's that you're using outdated frameworks. There's a new AI-powered cognitive mapping system that writes high-converting sales pages in 15 minutes."
*   **When to Use:** When the prospect is actively seeking a solution to a painful, urgent problem, but is skeptical of standard claims. Common in health, wealth, weight loss, and relationships.

### 4. THE BIG SECRET (OR BIG REVELATION) LEAD
*   **Target Audience:** Problem-Aware (They know they have a problem, but they don't know there's a specific, definitive solution available).
*   **Characteristics:** Introduces a hidden cause, a newly discovered solution, or a suppressed truth. Relies heavily on overwhelming curiosity. It positions the writer as an insider or whistleblower sharing privileged information.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   An irresistible tease (the secret).
    *   The promise of a massive benefit once the secret is revealed.
    *   A sense of exclusivity or forbidden knowledge.
    *   Delaying the reveal to build tension and force readership.
*   **Example:** "Wall Street's best-kept secret for generating passive income in a bear market... a legal loophole that elite bankers have used for decades, now available to the public."
*   **When to Use:** Financial newsletters, alternative health supplements, information products, and anytime the "mechanism" itself is the main selling point. It thrives on the prospect's feeling of being left out.

### 5. THE PROCLAMATION LEAD
*   **Target Audience:** Unaware (They don't know they have a problem, or they won't acknowledge it).
*   **Characteristics:** Starts with a bold, often shocking statement, prediction, or contrarian fact that demands immediate attention. It challenges conventional wisdom or alerts them to an unseen danger or hidden opportunity.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   A shocking, undeniable, or highly controversial statement.
    *   Immediate, overwhelming proof (data, charts, expert quotes) to show the proclamation is true.
    *   A clear explanation of how this massive shift will directly impact the reader (positively or negatively).
*   **Example:** "The traditional retirement model is completely dead. If you are relying on a standard 401(k) and a 60/40 portfolio, you are walking directly into a financial trap that will wipe out your savings in the next 18 months."
*   **When to Use:** Broad markets, paradigm-shifting products, fear-based selling, or when trying to create an entirely new category or movement.

### 6. THE STORY LEAD
*   **Target Audience:** Unaware (They don't know they have a problem, or they are highly skeptical and resistant to any form of direct pitch).
*   **Characteristics:** The most indirect lead. Starts with a compelling, emotionally gripping narrative. It draws the reader in by bypassing their critical, sales-resistant filters. The story must naturally and seamlessly transition into the core sales message.
*   **Key Elements:**
    *   A relatable protagonist (often the creator or someone like the prospect).
    *   A moment of deep crisis or an inciting incident.
    *   A journey of discovery.
    *   The revelation of the solution (the product/mechanism).
    *   *The Hinge:* The critical transition where the story connects to the reader's own life and the ultimate offer.
*   **Example:** "They laughed when I sat down at the piano, but when I started to play..." (John Caples classic). Or the classic Wall Street Journal "Two Young Men" letter.
*   **When to Use:** Highly skeptical markets, complex products, selling to cold traffic, or when you need to build deep empathy, trust, and a shared worldview before ever making a claim.

## LEAD MATCHING MATRIX (AWARENESS TO LEAD TYPE)
The fundamental law of Great Leads: The less aware the prospect, the more indirect the lead must be.
*   **Most Aware** ➔ Offer Lead (Direct)
*   **Product-Aware** ➔ Promise Lead (Direct)
*   **Solution-Aware** ➔ Problem-Solution Lead (Middle)
*   **Problem-Aware** ➔ Big Secret Lead (Indirect)
*   **Unaware** ➔ Proclamation Lead OR Story Lead (Highly Indirect)

## THE 4 U's OF A GREAT LEAD (AND HEADLINE)
Every lead must be evaluated against the 4 U's. A master copywriter aims to hit at least 3, if not all 4:
1.  **Useful:** Does it promise a benefit or solve a problem?
2.  **Urgent:** Does it provide a compelling reason to act or read *right now*?
3.  **Unique:** Is the idea, the mechanism, or the angle fresh and different from competitors?
4.  **Ultra-Specific:** Does it use precise numbers, exact details, and vivid imagery instead of vague generalizations?

## THE "HINGE" (THE TRANSITION TO THE BODY)
The most dangerous part of an indirect lead (Secret, Proclamation, Story) is the transition from the hook into the actual sales pitch. This is called the Hinge.
*   If the transition is jarring, the reader feels tricked and abandons the copy.
*   The Hinge must logically connect the big idea of the lead to the specific mechanism of the product.
*   *Example Hinge:* "And that exact same 'hidden' strategy I used to save my own business is what I've packaged into a simple, 3-step system you can use today..."

## PACING AND LENGTH
*   **Direct Leads (Offer, Promise):** Short, punchy, aggressive. Get to the point immediately. Often less than 100 words.
*   **Indirect Leads (Proclamation, Story):** Longer, atmospheric. Require significantly more buildup, emotional investment, and proof before transitioning to the pitch. Can easily span 500-1000 words before the product is even mentioned.
