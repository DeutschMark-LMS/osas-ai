# Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene M. Schwartz — DMA Master Playbook

## The First Law of Advertising: Mass Desire
- **The Core Axiom:** Copy cannot create desire for a product. It can only channel, direct, and focus the hopes, dreams, fears, and desires that *already exist* in the hearts of millions of people onto a particular product.
- **The Copywriter's Role:** You are a "Market-Maker" and a conceptual midwife. Trying to educate a market or create a desire that doesn't exist is financial suicide.

---

## PART I: THE 5 STATES OF CUSTOMER AWARENESS

Every headline and lead must meet the prospect at their exact point of awareness:

```
Unaware  ──>  Problem Aware  ──>  Solution Aware  ──>  Product Aware  ──>  Most Aware
(Identity)     (Dramatize Need)   (New Mechanism)     (Superiority)         (Price / Deal)
```

### 1. Most Aware
- **State of Mind:** The prospect knows your product, knows what it does, and knows they want it. They just haven't bought yet.
- **Headline Strategy:** Product name + price/offer/discount:
  - *Example:* "Revere Zomar Lens Camera—Formerly $149.50—Now Only $119.95."

### 2. Product Aware (Knows Product, Doesn't Yet Want It)
- **State of Mind:** They know your brand/product, but aren't fully convinced or don't know why it's better.
- **Headline Strategy:** Display product name and prove superiority across 7 tasks:
  1. *Reinforce desire* via Association ("Steinway—The Instrument of the Immortals").
  2. *Sharpen sensory image* of performance ("At 60 mph, loudest noise in this Rolls Royce is the electric clock").
  3. *Extend time/place* of use ("Thirst knows no season"—Coca-Cola).
  4. *Introduce proof/documentation* ("9 out of 10 screen stars use Lux Soap").
  5. *Announce new mechanism* ("Hoover's new invention washes floors and vacuums up scrub water").
  6. *Eliminate former limitation* ("You breathe no dusty odors with Lewyt").
  7. *Change image/mechanism* to escape competition.

### 3. Solution Aware (Knows The Result Desired, Not Your Product)
- **State of Mind:** They know what outcome they want, but don't know your specific product exists.
- **Headline Strategy:** Pinpoint the desire, crystalize the solution, and introduce the mechanism:
  - *Examples:* "How to Win Friends and Influence People"; "Now! Run Your Car Without Spark Plugs"; "Who Else Wants a Whiter Wash—With No Hard Work?"

### 4. Problem Aware (Feels The Pain/Need, Doesn't Know A Solution Exists)
- **State of Mind:** They have an acute bleeding neck problem, but haven't identified the solution vehicle yet.
- **Headline Strategy:** Name the problem/symptom, dramatize the pain, and promise relief:
  - *Examples:* "Corns?"; "Stops Maddening Itch"; "Do YOU Make These Mistakes in English?"; "Shrinks Hemorrhoids Without Surgery."

### 5. Completely Unaware
- **State of Mind:** Completely indifferent. They don't know you, don't know the problem, or refuse to admit it.
- **Golden Rule:** **Never mention price, product, function, or desire in the headline.**
- **Headline Strategy:** Write an **Identification Headline**. Echo an attitude, a shared dream, a secret fear, or an unvoiced resentment that gathers your market together:
  - *Giving words to hidden dream:* "The University of the Night."
  - *Exploiting hidden fear:* "Why Men Crack…"
  - *Universally accepted image leading to taboo:* "Within the Curve of a Woman's Arm" (Deodorant).
  - *Unvoiced resentment:* "Why Haven't TV Owners Been Told These Facts?"
  - *Ultimate triumph/status:* "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano. But When I Started to Play!—"
  - *Projecting consequences:* "Often a Bridesmaid, But Never a Bride" (Listerine).

## PART II: THE 5 STAGES OF MARKET SOPHISTICATION

How many similar products have been presented to this market before you?

| Stage | Market Condition | Winning Copy Strategy | Classic Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage 1** | First in the market. Prospect is virgin. | Be simple, direct, state the raw claim. | "NOW! LOSE UGLY FAT!" |
| **Stage 2** | Competitors enter. Claim is established. | Enlarge the claim. Push to outer limits of believability. | "LOSE UP TO 47 LBS IN 4 WEEKS—OR RECEIVE $40 BACK!" |
| **Stage 3** | Claims are exhausted. Cynicism sets in. | **Introduce a NEW MECHANISM.** Shift focus from *what* to *HOW*. | "FLOATS FAT RIGHT OUT OF YOUR BODY!" / "FIRST WONDER DRUG FOR REDUCING!" |
| **Stage 4** | Competitors copy your mechanism. | Elaborate & improve the mechanism. Make it faster/easier. | "FIRST NO-DIET REDUCING WONDER DRUG!" |
| **Stage 5** | Market is dead, burned-out, exhausted. | Shift from claim/mechanism to **IDENTIFICATION & EMOTION**. | Marlboro Man / "Why Men Crack…" / "Blow Some My Way." |

## PART III: THE 38 WAYS TO STRENGTHEN A HEADLINE (VERBALIZATION)

When you know the basic claim, amplify it using these 38 proven formulas:
1. **Measure size of claim:** *"20,000 Filter Traps in Viceroy!"*
2. **Measure speed:** *"Feel Better Fast!"* / *"In Two Seconds, Bayer Aspirin Begins to Dissolve!"*
3. **Compare claim:** *"Six Times Whiter Washes!"*
4. **Metaphorize:** *"Melts Away Ugly Fat!"*
5. **Sensitize (Sensory):** *"Tastes Like You Just Picked It!"* / *"The Skin You Love to Touch!"*
6. **Demonstrate prime example:** *"At 60 mph, Loudest Noise in This Rolls Royce is the Electric Clock."*
7. **Dramatize result:** *"Here's an Extra $50, Grace—I'm Making Big Money Now!"*
8. **State as paradox:** *"How a Bald-Headed Barber Saved My Hair!"*
9. **Remove limitations:** *"Shrinks Hemorrhoids Without Surgery!"*
10. **Associate with authority/prestige:** *"9 Out of 10 Decorators Use Wunda-Weave Carpets!"*
11. **Detail amount of work done:** *"Relief From All 5 Acid-Caused Stomach Troubles in Seconds!"*
12. **State as question:** *"Who Else Wants a Whiter Wash—With No Hard Work?"*
13. **Offer how-to information:** *"How to Win Friends and Influence People."*
14. **Tie authority into claim:** *"Here's What Doctors Do When They Feel Rotten!"*
15. **Before-and-after contrast:** *"Before Coldene... With Coldene..."*
16. **Stress newness:** *"Announcing! Guided Missile Spark Plugs!"*
17. **Stress exclusivity:** *"Only Gleem Has GL-70!"*
18. **Turn claim into challenge:** *"Which Twin Has the Toni? And Which Has the $15 Permanent?"*
19. **Case history quotation:** *"Look, Mom—No Cavities!"*
20. **Condense / Interchange product:** *"Now! A Ring and Piston Job in a Tube!"*
21. **Symbolize claim:** *"Starting Next Tuesday, The Atlantic Ocean Becomes Only 1/5th as Long!"*
22. **Connect mechanism to claim:** *"Floats Fat Right Out of Your Body!"*
23. **Startle by contradicting expectations:** *"Hit Hell Out of the Ball With Your Right Hand!"*
24. **Connect need and claim:** *"Only One Solution to Advertising: Find The Man!"*
25. **Offer information in ad:** *"What Everybody Ought to Know About This Stock & Bond Business!"*
26. **Turn need into case history:** *"Again She Orders—A Chicken Salad, Please."*
27. **Name the problem/symptom:** *"When You're Weary With Daytime Fatigue, Take Alka-Seltzer."*
28. **Warn of pitfalls:** *"Don't Invest One Cent Until You Check This Guide!"*
29. **Emphasize by repetition/cadence:** *"Nobody But Nobody Undersells Gimbel's!"*
30. **Show ease via trivial limitation:** *"If You Can Count to Eleven, You Can Master Speed Math!"*
31. **State the difference:** *"The Difference in Premium Gasolines is in the Additives!"*
32. **Surprise by overcoming limits:** *"See What Happens When You Crush a Hartman Luggage? Nothing!"*
33. **Address those who can't buy:** *"If You've Already Taken Your Vacation, Don't Read This."*
34. **Address prospect directly:** *"To The Man Who Will Settle for Nothing Less Than The Presidency."*
35. **Dramatize production difficulty:** *"We Had to Invent a Whole New Kind of Candle."*
36. **Accuse claim of being too good:** *"Is It Immoral to Make Money This Easily?"*
37. **Challenge limiting beliefs:** *"You Are Twice as Smart as You Think."*
38. **Question and Answer:** *"Who Should You See If Your Car Stalls? Someone Who Cares."*

## PART IV: THE 7 BASIC TECHNIQUES OF BREAKTHROUGH BODY COPY

How to write body copy that matches the power of your headline:

### 1. Intensification (13 Ways to Strengthen Desire)
1. *Blunt Detailed Description:* Paint sensory detail of results.
2. *Put Product in Action:* Walk the reader through what happens the second they use it.
3. *Put Reader in the Middle:* "Picture this to yourself: Next weekend you go down to your car..."
4. *Turn Demonstration into a Test:* Step-by-step experiment they can perform themselves.
5. *Stretch Benefits in Time:* Show continuous compounding rewards across weeks, months, and years.
6. *Bring in an Audience:* Show spouses, neighbors, and competitors gasping in admiration.
7. *Show Experts Approving:* "The astonishment of experts" carries supreme emotional weight.
8. *Compare, Contrast & Prove Superiority:* Put the old and new methods side-by-side.
9. *Picture the Black Side (Heaven or Hell):* Irritate the wound, paint the misery of staying the same, then apply the healing balm.
10. *Show How Easy It Is:* "30 seconds of work..."
11. *Use Metaphor & Analogy:* Personify inanimate tools (e.g. Sherwin Cody's "100% Self-Correcting Device at your elbow").
12. *Summary / Catalog:* The bulleted laundry list of benefits and applications ("Does 101 jobs...").
13. *Put Your Guarantee to Work:* Transform the guarantee into the climactic summary of your offer.

### 2. Identification (Character & Achievement Roles)
- People do not buy products; they buy **roles** and **social identity**.
- **Character Roles (Adjectives):** Progressive, virile, brilliant, chic, charming.
- **Achievement Roles (Status Nouns):** Executive, Homeowner, Partner, Winner.
- Every ad must give the prospect a vehicle to express who they are or who they want to become.

### 3. Gradualization (The Architecture of Belief)
- You cannot force a prospect to accept a claim they aren't prepared for.
- **The Yes-Train:** Build a continuous stream of acceptances using the **6 Structural Belief Devices**:
  1. **The Inclusion Question:** A question 95% of your target market answers "Yes" to (e.g. *"Was your set purchased after 1947?"* or *"Do you find it increasingly difficult to cope with the world around you?"*). Places the reader in the agreement group immediately.
  2. **Detailed Identification of Symptoms:** Cataloging exact daily frustrations so the reader thinks *"This person has been living inside my head"* (*"How many times this week have you had to get up to fix a jumpy picture?"*).
  3. **Contradiction of Present (False) Beliefs:** Loosening grip on old limitations (*"From this moment on, forget everything you've ever heard about what age 'must do' to your skin..."*).
  4. **The Language of Logic:** Words that carry centuries of court-proven conviction (*"The reason for this deficiency is clear..."*, *"Therefore..."*, *"Because..."*, *"This has been proved by thousands..."*).
  5. **Syllogistic Thinking:** Step-by-step chains of cause and effect (e.g., *Spark size $\to$ explosion power $\to$ horsepower $\to$ gas mileage*). Creates a feeling of absolute inevitability.
  6. **Contingency Structures & Paragraph Parallelism:** Structuring statements as *"If... then..."* and repeating proven sentence rhythms to borrow trust.
- One fully-believed claim has 10x the power of ten partially-believed claims.

### 4. Redefinition (Concept-Judo)
- How to turn product liabilities into assets:
  - *Simplification:* Redefine "repairs" as simple "external adjustments."
  - *Escalation:* Elevate a grammar course into an essential leadership and income persuasion tool.
  - *Price Reduction via Comparison Standards:* Compare a $2,000 course not to a book, but to a $50,000 university degree.

### 5. Mechanization (Verbally Proving HOW It Works)
- Three levels:
  1. *Name the Mechanism* (for unsophisticated markets).
  2. *Describe the Mechanism* (Promise $\to$ Reason-Why).
  3. *Feature the Mechanism in the Headline* (for sophisticated markets).
- Price-cut mechanization (give an airtight, logical reason *why* you are offering a discount, e.g., Robert Collier surplus inventory story).

### 6. Concentration (Destroying Competitors & Alternatives)
- Point out weaknesses in conventional methods, emphasize their disservice to the prospect, and show how your product solves it without the drawback.
- **Bad-Good Contrast Structure:** Spark plug burns out $\to$ Fire Injector never burns out.

### 7. Camouflage (Borrowing Conviction)
- Make ads look like editorial content, news reports, or insider memos.
- **Understatement & Deadly Sincerity:** Point out the flaws and sacrifices required first ("This is not meant for 99 out of 100 men...") to make the promised transformation 100% believable.

## PART V: CREATIVE PLANNING & SPECIAL STRATEGIES

### 1. The Three Levels of Creativity
1. **Level 1 (Word-Substitution):** The shallowest level. Taking a famous headline and swapping words (e.g., *"I'd walk a mile for a cup of [Brand] coffee"*). Results in weak "Echo Ads."
2. **Level 2 (Formulas):** Pouring ideas into rigid molds. Helpful for verbalization, but limits true innovation.
3. **Level 3 (Analysis):** The true breakthrough method. Deeply analyzing the unique product-market-timing relationship that has never existed before.

### 2. The ONLY Type of Prevention Headline That Sells
- **The Psychology of Denial:** A prospect will **never** visualize a future catastrophe happening to themselves (e.g. heart attack, premature death). They reject it unconsciously.
- **The Golden Exception:** Prospects are terrified of disasters happening to **their loved ones, spouse, and children**.
  - Decay-preventing toothpaste sells by showing the decay in the *child's* teeth, not the parent's.
  - Life insurance sells on the financial destitution inflicted on the *wife and children*, not the man's mortality.

### 3. Splinter Markets to Avoid Competition
- Instead of competing head-on against giant budgets, carve out a sociological splinter market:
  - E.g. Weight loss for *appearance* vs. Weight loss for *health & longevity*.
  - Frame the mechanism, tone, and proof specifically for the splinter group.

## PART VI: ADVANCED COPY DYNAMICS & SHIFT POINTS

### The 3 Inevitable Reader Demands:
As a prospect reads your copy, their mind constantly cycles through three demands:
1. **"Tell me more"** (Demand for intensified desire/benefit).
2. **"Oh yeah? Who says so?"** (Demand for proof/documentation).
3. **"How does it work?"** (Demand for mechanism).
- **The Sensitivity Rule:** You must anticipate the exact point in the copy where the reader transitions from wanting promises to demanding proof or mechanism. If you pile on promises when they demand proof, the copy dies.

### Reinforcement (1 + 1 = 10) & Interweaving:
- **Juxtaposition:** Join two emotional images so their impact multiplies tenfold (*"We're only second. Therefore, we try harder"*).
- **Interweaving:** Pack promise, logic, and emotion into the exact same sentence.
- **Momentum Phrases:** Continuous slippery-slope connectors (*"And yet it's only the beginning..."; "Let me explain..."; "What you will discover in the first hour is this..."*).
